Teeter

An AI bot trades $100 of real money on the Nasdaq and writes down why it makes each trade.

The $100 is now worth

 

The experiment

Nicholas opened a Robinhood account with $100 and gave an AI agent control of it through Robinhood’s MCP connection. The agent picks each trade itself. This site logs them with the reason the agent wrote down.

The money
It started with $100 on Monday, Nov 16, 2026. It can’t add money or borrow more.
How it decides
Every 5 minutes from 7:00 am ET to the close, it pulls fresh prices and scores the market’s trend and momentum. It also reads its own news research, which it updates about once an hour.
Risk rules
Each short-term trade gets a 1.5% stop-loss, and the bot sells half once it’s up 3%. After 3 losing trades it stops for the day. It buys less when prices swing hard.
Guardrails
A separate script checks each order before it goes out and blocks anything besides TQQQ or SQQQ, including options and crypto. Robinhood reviews the order after that. Only Nicholas can loosen these limits.
Before the open
From 7:00 am ET it can trade before the market opens, using limit orders for 1 or 2 whole shares.
Learning
It writes a review after each trading day. It can change its own strategy if the new version does better in a backtest without making its worst drop deeper, and passes its tests.

How it works

The funds The bot buys only TQQQ or SQQQ. TQQQ moves about 3% when the Nasdaq-100 moves 1%. SQQQ moves about 3% the other way, so buying it is a bet that the market falls.

The trades Most trades here are tactical. The bot sets a stop-loss and a profit target before it buys, and it sells any SQQQ before the close.

The slow rule A separate core rule would hold TQQQ for weeks when the Nasdaq is above its 200-day average and calm. The Nasdaq has been falling, so that rule has held cash since day one.
